How to Spot Fake Sellers and Stay Safe on Facebook Marketplace and OLX (2026 Guide)

Last updated: 20 November 2026

By CyberSec Hub · 18 min read
Ethical Use Only: Only test systems you own or have written permission to test. Unauthorized hacking is illegal in Pakistan and worldwide. Everything here is for defensive security education and authorized testing only.

You find the phone you have wanted for months on Facebook Marketplace — an iPhone 13 Pro, barely used, at half the market price. The seller replies within minutes, sounds friendly, and says three other buyers are waiting. There is just one condition: send a small advance payment through JazzCash to "book" it, and he will deliver it to your doorstep. You send the money. The seller's profile disappears, your messages go unanswered, and the phone never arrives. This is the fake seller scam, and it is one of the most common ways people lose money online in Pakistan. Facebook Marketplace and OLX are genuine, useful places to buy and sell — thousands of honest people use them every day — but they are also hunting grounds for criminals who create fake listings, steal photos from real sellers, and pressure buyers into paying before they have seen anything. A fake seller does not need to be a technical genius. All they need is a stolen photo, a price that is too good to ignore, and a buyer in a hurry. The good news is that fake sellers follow predictable scripts, and once you know them, you can spot a scam listing in under a minute. This guide explains the fake-seller scams running on Marketplace and OLX in Pakistan right now, the warning signs, the practical steps to check any seller, how to meet and pay safely, and exactly what to do if you have already been scammed.

What is a fake seller scam?

A fake seller scam is a listing for something that does not exist, does not belong to the seller, or will never be delivered. The criminal's goal is simple: get your money before you realise the item is not real. The scam usually works in one of three ways:

  • The advance payment trap. The seller asks for an advance — a "booking fee", "token money", or "delivery charge" — paid through JazzCash, Easypaisa, or a bank transfer before you have seen the item. Once the money is sent, the seller blocks you and vanishes. This is the most common version of the scam in Pakistan, because mobile wallet payments are instant and very hard to reverse.
  • The fake delivery. The seller says the item is in another city and will be sent by courier after you pay. They may even send a tracking number or a photo of a parcel. The parcel never arrives, or it contains something worthless — a brick, an old broken phone, or an empty box. Our SMS scams guide explains the fake-parcel message trick, which is often part of the same scam.
  • The stolen item. Sometimes the item is real, but the seller does not own it. They copy photos and details from a genuine listing, sell the same item to several people at once, and collect advance payments from all of them. Or the item itself is stolen, and buying it can put you in trouble with the police.

Fake sellers succeed because buying online feels normal now, and because a great deal creates excitement. Excitement makes people skip the checks they would never skip in a shop. The scammer's whole job is to keep you excited and in a hurry until the money is sent.

The 8 most common fake-seller scams on Marketplace and OLX in Pakistan

Most fake listings in Pakistan are variations of the same eight scripts. Learn them once, and you will recognise them every time:

  • 1. The too-good-to-be-true price. A new iPhone for Rs. 40,000, a 125 motorcycle for Rs. 50,000, a branded laptop for a quarter of its shop price. The price is the bait. Nobody sells a working phone for half its value to a stranger — if the deal looks impossible, the seller is not generous, they are a criminal. Always check the real market price first: search the same item on OLX, in Daraz, and in a local shop before you believe any listing.
  • 2. The advance payment or "token money" demand. "Send Rs. 5,000 advance to confirm, the item is yours." Or: "Many buyers are asking, whoever pays first gets it." A genuine seller who has the item in hand has no reason to demand money before you have seen it. Any request for payment before inspection is the single biggest red flag in this guide. Our fake loan apps guide shows the same advance-fee trick in a different disguise.
  • 3. The fake payment receipt. You are the seller, and the "buyer" sends you a screenshot of a bank transfer or JazzCash payment that never arrives in your account. The screenshot is edited or fake, and the buyer pressures you to hand over the item or send the "courier fee" first. Never release an item on the strength of a screenshot — check your own bank app or wallet balance first.
  • 4. The courier and delivery scam. The seller is "in another city" and will send the item by courier after payment. They may share a fake tracking link or ask you to pay a "courier company" through a link they send. Real courier companies do not collect payment through links sent by a seller. If you cannot see the item in person, do not pay for it.
  • 5. Stolen photos and copied listings. The scammer copies photos and the description from a real seller's listing — sometimes from another city or even another country — and reposts them at a lower price. The photos look professional because they were taken by the real owner. Ask the seller for a new photo of the item with today's date written on a piece of paper next to it, or a short live video call showing the item. A real owner can do this in a minute; a scammer cannot, because they do not have the item.
  • 6. The "army officer" or "abroad" seller. "I am an army officer posted in another city, so I cannot meet. I will courier it after payment." Or: "I am abroad, my brother will deliver it." A respected job title, a uniform photo, or a story about being abroad is meant to make you trust the seller and skip your checks. A job title is not a guarantee — judge the deal, not the story. Our fake job offers guide explains how scammers use impressive titles to build false trust.
  • 7. The fake OLX or Facebook verification link. The seller sends you a link and says "click here to verify the deal on OLX" or "complete payment on this secure page". The link opens a fake website that looks like OLX or Facebook and asks for your bank details, card number, or OTP. OLX and Facebook never ask you to complete a payment through a link sent in chat. Our guide to spotting fake websites shows how to check a link before you trust it, and our phishing protection guide explains the fake-website trick in full.
  • 8. The rental and property advance. A beautiful house or shop for rent at half the area's normal rent, with photos copied from a property website. The "owner" asks for one month's advance to "reserve" it before viewing. Property scams use the same script as phone scams — an unbelievably good price plus pressure to pay before seeing. Never pay rent or an advance for a property you have not visited in person.

10 red flags that a seller is fake

You do not need to memorise every scam script. These ten signs are enough to catch almost all of them:

  • A price far below the market. If the same item sells for Rs. 100,000 everywhere else and this listing says Rs. 45,000, something is wrong. Check the market price before you message the seller.
  • Pressure and urgency. "Pay now or I will sell it to someone else", "offer ends today", "three buyers are waiting". Real sellers can wait a day; scammers cannot, because a careful buyer is their worst enemy.
  • Payment before inspection. Any request for advance, token money, booking fee, or delivery charges before you have seen the item. This is the clearest sign of a scam.
  • Refusal to meet in person. The seller is always "out of city", "busy", or "only does delivery". A genuine local seller can usually meet you, or show the item on a video call.
  • A new or empty profile. The Facebook account was created this month, has almost no friends, no photos, and no history. On OLX, the account has no reviews and was created days ago. Check the profile before you trust the listing.
  • Stolen or stock photos. The photos look too perfect, or the same photos appear in other listings in other cities. Do a reverse image search: save the photo and search it on Google Images. If the same photo appears in many different listings, it is stolen.
  • Payment only by JazzCash, Easypaisa, or bank transfer. The seller refuses cash on delivery and insists on a mobile wallet or bank transfer. Wallet payments are instant and hard to trace or reverse — that is exactly why scammers prefer them.
  • Chat moves off the platform. The seller immediately asks you to continue on WhatsApp and refuses to talk in Marketplace or OLX chat. Off-platform chat removes the platform's protection and evidence trail.
  • Requests for secret information. The seller or "buyer" asks for your OTP, bank card number, or account password — for "verification" or "payment". No genuine buyer or seller ever needs your OTP or password. Our SMS scams guide explains why your OTP must never be shared with anyone.
  • Bad grammar, copied text, or mismatched details. The description says the phone is "brand new" but the photos show scratches; the location says Lahore but the seller claims to be in Karachi; the listing text is copied word for word from another ad. Small contradictions are a strong warning.

If a listing shows even one of these signs, slow down and check. If it shows two or more, walk away — no deal is worth losing your money.

Step 1: Check the seller before you message them

Spend two minutes checking the seller before you start negotiating. It is the cheapest protection you have:

  • On Facebook Marketplace, open the seller's profile. When was the account created? Does it have real photos, friends, and normal posts? A profile created last week with one photo and no friends is a warning sign. Check whether the seller has other listings — a real seller often has a history of buying and selling. Check the seller's ratings and reviews if they have any.
  • On OLX, check the member-since date and reviews. OLX shows how long the account has existed and whether other buyers left feedback. An account that is days old with no reviews deserves extra caution. OLX also shows whether the phone number is verified — prefer verified sellers.
  • Search the seller's name, number, and photos. Paste the seller's phone number into Google or Facebook search. Scammers reuse numbers, and other victims often report them online. Do a reverse image search on the listing photos: if the same photos appear under different names or in different cities, they are stolen.
  • Ask questions only the real owner can answer. When did you buy it? Where is the original box and receipt? Why are you selling it? Can you show the item on a video call right now? A real owner answers easily and can show the item live. A scammer gives vague answers, changes the story, or refuses a video call.

Step 2: Never pay in advance — inspect first, pay after

This is the single most important rule in this guide. Money leaves your hand once, and on JazzCash, Easypaisa, or a bank transfer it is almost impossible to get back. Follow these rules:

  • Never send advance, token money, or a booking fee. Not Rs. 500, not Rs. 5,000 — no amount. A genuine seller with the item in hand does not need your money before you have seen it. "Token money to confirm" is the fake seller's favourite phrase, and it is always a scam.
  • Pay only after you have held the item in your hands. Cash on delivery, in person, after inspection. If the seller insists on payment first, the deal is over — walk away.
  • If you are selling, never hand over the item on a screenshot. Wait until the money actually appears in your own bank app or wallet. A screenshot of a payment proves nothing — screenshots can be faked in seconds. Check your own balance, not their picture.
  • Never share your OTP, PIN, or card details to "receive" a payment. Receiving money never requires your OTP or password. Anyone who asks for your code while "paying" you is stealing from you, not paying you.
  • Keep all chat on the platform. Stay in Facebook Marketplace or OLX chat, where the conversation is recorded. If something goes wrong, that chat history is your evidence. A seller who insists on moving to WhatsApp immediately is usually trying to escape the platform's records.

Step 3: Meet in a safe, public place

If the deal is real, the seller can meet you. How and where you meet matters as much as the price:

  • Meet in daylight, in a busy public place. A shopping mall, a busy market, a bank branch, or outside a police station. Never meet at the seller's home, your home, or a quiet street at night. Tell a family member or friend where you are going, who you are meeting, and when you expect to be back.
  • Bring someone with you. Two people are safer than one, especially for expensive items like phones, laptops, and motorcycles. A scammer who planned to rob you or pressure you will think twice when you are not alone.
  • For vehicles, meet at a proper place and check the papers. For a motorcycle or car, meet in daylight and check the original registration book, the seller's CNIC, and whether the name on the papers matches the seller. Check the engine and chassis numbers against the registration book. Never buy a vehicle without seeing its original documents.
  • Trust your feeling. If the seller keeps changing the meeting place, arrives late with excuses, brings a different item from the photos, or pressures you to hurry, leave. A real seller understands caution; a scammer hates it.

Step 4: Inspect the item like a buyer, not a fan

Excitement is the scammer's best friend. Slow down and check the item properly before you pay:

  • For phones: turn it on, check the IMEI number (dial *#06# and compare it with the number on the box), test the camera, speakers, charging port, and fingerprint sensor. Check that the phone is not iCloud-locked or Google-locked — ask the seller to sign out of their accounts in front of you and reset the phone. A phone still linked to someone else's account can be locked remotely after you pay.
  • For laptops and electronics: switch them on, check the battery health, test the keyboard, screen, and ports, and confirm the model number matches the listing. Ask for the original charger and box.
  • For anything expensive: ask for the original receipt, warranty card, or box. A seller who has the receipt is far more likely to be genuine. If the item is "brand new" but has no box, no receipt, and no warranty, ask why.
  • Compare the item with the photos. If the listing showed a clean phone and the real one is scratched, cracked, or a different colour, the photos were stolen. Walk away — the seller has already lied to you once.

Step 5: Use Marketplace and OLX safety features

Both platforms give you tools to stay safe. Use them:

  • Stay in the platform's chat and payment system. Facebook Marketplace and OLX keep a record of your conversation. If the seller scams you, that record is the evidence you need to report them.
  • Report fake listings. On Facebook Marketplace, open the listing, tap the three dots, and choose "Report listing". On OLX, use the "Report this ad" option. Reporting takes seconds, and it protects the next buyer. Report the seller's profile too if it is clearly fake.
  • Block the scammer. After reporting, block the account so they cannot message you again with a new fake listing.
  • Check for verified badges and ratings. Prefer sellers with verified phone numbers, older accounts, and positive reviews from other buyers. A seller with a long, honest history has something to lose; a brand-new account has nothing to lose.
  • Learn the platform's rules. Facebook and OLX both publish safety guidance for buyers and sellers. Read it once — it takes five minutes and it tells you exactly what the platform can and cannot do if a deal goes wrong. Our safe online shopping guide covers the wider rules for buying safely on any website or app.

Step 6: If you were scammed — act fast

It happens to careful people too. If you sent money to a fake seller, do not just feel embarrassed and stay silent — act quickly, in this order:

  1. Save all evidence immediately. Take screenshots of the listing, the seller's profile, the full chat, the payment receipt, the phone number, and any bank or wallet transaction details. Screenshots are your evidence — without them, reporting is much harder.
  2. Contact your bank or wallet provider at once. If you paid by bank transfer, call your bank immediately and report the fraud — ask whether the transfer can be stopped or recalled. If you paid through JazzCash or Easypaisa, call their helpline and report the transaction the same day. The first hour matters most.
  3. Report the seller on the platform. Report the listing and the seller's profile on Facebook Marketplace or OLX, so the account is removed before it scams someone else.
  4. Report the crime to the FIA Cybercrime Wing. Online fraud and advance-payment scams can be reported to the FIA. Keep your screenshots, the seller's number, and your payment proof ready. Our guide to reporting cybercrime and online scams gives the exact steps and the evidence to keep.
  5. Report the number to the PTA. You can report a scam number by forwarding the details to the PTA. Reporting the number helps get it blocked.
  6. Warn others. If the scammer used a Facebook group or an OLX listing, warn the group admin or post a careful warning so other buyers are not trapped. Do not share the scammer's personal details publicly beyond what is needed to warn others — stick to the facts of the listing and the number used.
  7. Learn from it and move on. Being scammed is not a reason to stop buying online — it is a reason to follow the rules in this guide next time. Every experienced online buyer in Pakistan has a story like this. The lesson is cheap compared with the next scam it prevents.

What a real seller never does

Keep this list on your phone, or teach it to your family. If a seller does any of these, the listing is fake:

  • A real seller never asks for advance, token money, or a booking fee before you have seen the item.
  • A real seller never refuses to meet in person or show the item on a live video call.
  • A real seller never sends you a link to "verify" the deal or complete payment on another website.
  • A real seller never asks for your OTP, PIN, password, or card number.
  • A real seller never pressures you to pay now or lose the deal.
  • A real seller never uses stolen photos or a profile created yesterday.
  • A real seller's price is close to the market price — nobody sells a working phone for half its value to a stranger.

Teach this list to your parents, your children, and anyone in your family who buys or sells online. The people scammers target most are the ones nobody warned — and a five-minute conversation with your family can save them from losing their savings.

Key Takeaways

  • Fake sellers sell items they do not have — using stolen photos, impossible prices, and pressure to pay in advance on Facebook Marketplace and OLX.
  • Never pay in advance. Inspect the item in person first, then pay — cash on delivery, after you have held it in your hands.
  • Check the seller before you trust the listing: profile age, reviews, verified number, reverse image search, and a live video call.
  • Watch for the warning signs: a price far below the market, urgency and pressure, refusal to meet, a new or empty profile, and payment only by JazzCash, Easypaisa, or bank transfer.
  • Meet in daylight in a busy public place, bring someone with you, and inspect the item properly before paying.
  • Keep chat on the platform and never share your OTP, PIN, or password with any buyer or seller.
  • If you were scammed, save the evidence, call your bank or wallet provider immediately, report the seller, and report the crime to the FIA.
  • Everything here is defensive and legal — protecting your own money and your own family from scammers.
