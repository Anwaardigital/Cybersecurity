How to Secure Your Telegram Account: Stop Scams, Hacks and Fake Channels (2026 Guide)

Last updated: 17 November 2026

By CyberSec Hub · 11 min read
Ethical Use Only: Only test systems you own or have written permission to test. Unauthorized hacking is illegal in Pakistan and worldwide. Everything here is for defensive security education and authorized testing only.

Telegram has become one of the most popular messaging apps in Pakistan — used for family groups, study groups, business channels, news, and crypto communities. That popularity is exactly why criminals love it. Telegram's open channels, big groups, and bot ecosystem give scammers a huge hunting ground: fake channels that copy real ones, "free crypto" giveaways, fake Telegram Premium offers, job scams, and investment groups full of fake winners. And a stolen Telegram account is valuable to a thief. With your account they can message your contacts pretending to be you, run scams in your name, steal your groups and channels, and read your private chats. The good news is that Telegram has strong built-in protections — two-step verification, active session controls, and fine-grained privacy settings — but most people never turn them on. This guide explains how Telegram accounts actually get stolen, the warning signs, and the exact settings to lock down today, plus what to do if your account is already stolen. Everything here is defensive: protecting your own account, your own chats, and your own contacts.

How Telegram accounts get stolen

Criminals rarely break into Telegram's servers. Instead, they trick you, or trick the systems around your account. These are the five methods used most often:

  • The login code trick (the most common). Telegram logs you in with a code sent by SMS or to your other Telegram device. The thief enters your phone number on their own phone, and Telegram sends you a login code. Then the thief messages you — pretending to be a friend, a delivery rider, or Telegram support — and asks you to forward that code "to confirm a delivery" or "to fix an error". The moment you share the code, they log in as you. Telegram will never ask you for your login code, and no genuine friend needs it.
  • Fake Telegram apps and modded APKs. "Telegram Plus", "Telegram Gold", "GB Telegram" and similar apps promise extra features, themes, or the ability to see deleted messages. Many of them are built to steal your login session the moment you sign in. Others are copies of the real app with hidden code that sends your messages to a thief. Only ever install Telegram from the official Google Play Store, Apple App Store, or telegram.org.
  • The QR code and "link a device" scam. The thief sends you a QR code — in a message, a poster, or a "free gift" link — and asks you to scan it with Telegram's "Link Desktop Device" feature. Scanning it links YOUR Telegram to THEIR computer, and they can read your chats in real time. Never scan a Telegram QR code sent to you by a stranger, and never scan one to "win" anything.
  • SIM swapping. The criminal convinces your mobile company to issue a replacement SIM for your number, or steals your phone and uses the SIM in another phone. With your number in their hands, they receive your Telegram login code themselves. Our SIM swapping protection guide explains how to stop this.
  • Fake support, Premium and giveaway messages. "Your Telegram account will be deleted in 24 hours, click here to verify." "You won 1 year of Telegram Premium, claim it now." "Free crypto giveaway, connect your wallet." These messages push you to a fake login page that steals your code, or ask you to pay a small "fee" to claim a prize that does not exist. Telegram does not delete accounts this way, and it does not give prizes by message.

7 warning signs your Telegram account is compromised

If you notice any of these, check your account immediately using the steps below:

  • You are logged out of Telegram suddenly and it asks for a login code again, even though you did nothing. This is the clearest sign someone else logged in with your number.
  • Messages appear as "read" that you never opened, or replies were sent from your account that you did not write.
  • Your contacts receive strange messages from you — asking for money, sharing links, or offering prizes. If a friend says "you sent me a weird link", believe them.
  • Your profile photo, name, or bio changed without you changing it, or new groups and channels appeared that you never joined.
  • You see an unknown session in your active sessions list — a computer, phone, or location you do not recognise.
  • A Telegram login code arrives when you did not request one. Someone just tried to log in as you. Do not share that code with anyone, and turn on two-step verification right now.
  • Your groups or channels have a new admin you did not add, or posts appear in your channel that you did not write.

If several of these apply, treat your account as stolen and jump to the recovery section at the end of this guide. Our guide to telling if your phone is hacked covers the wider warning signs that apply to your whole phone as well.

Step 1: Turn on two-step verification (the single most important step)

Two-step verification adds a password that anyone logging in with your number must enter — even if they have your SMS login code. Without it, a stolen code is enough to take your account. With it, the thief is stopped. Turn it on now; it takes less than a minute:

  1. Open Telegram, tap the three lines (Android) or Settings (iPhone), then Settings.
  2. Tap Privacy and Security, then Two-Step Verification.
  3. Tap Set Password and choose a strong password you will remember. Do not use your birthday, your phone number, or 123456 — pick something only you know. A password manager can store it safely; our password manager guide shows how.
  4. Add a password hint if you want one, and add your recovery email address when Telegram asks. This email lets you reset the password if you forget it. Use an email account you actually control, and make sure that email itself has a strong password and two-factor authentication.
  5. Confirm the password and the email. Done — your account is now far harder to steal.

Telegram will ask you for this password from time to time, so you do not forget it. If you ever forget the password and you did not add a recovery email, you will be locked out of your account — so the email step matters. Our two-factor authentication guide explains the same idea for your Google, Facebook and banking accounts.

Step 2: Never share your login code — with anyone

This is the one rule that stops most Telegram theft. The code Telegram sends by SMS or to your other device is the key to your account. Treat it like your bank card PIN:

  • Never share the code, even with someone you know. If a friend's account is stolen, the thief messages all of their contacts asking for codes — so the request may come from a real friend's number. If anyone asks for your Telegram code for any reason, it is a scam. A real friend never needs your code.
  • Never say the code out loud on a call. Scammers call pretending to be from a delivery company, a bank, or Telegram itself, and ask you to read the code that just arrived. Telegram staff will never call you and ask for a code.
  • Do not type the code into any website or form. A link that asks for your Telegram login code is a trap. Close it. The real Telegram login page is only at web.telegram.org or in the official app — never a link someone sent you.
  • If a code arrives and you did not ask for one, someone is trying to steal your account right now. Do not panic and do not share it. Open Telegram and check your active sessions (Step 3), and make sure two-step verification is on (Step 1).

Step 3: Check your active sessions every week

Telegram lets you use your account on several phones, tablets and computers at once. A thief who logs in on their computer can read your messages without you knowing — and you will not be logged out. Checking takes thirty seconds:

  1. Open Telegram, go to Settings, then Devices (on older versions: Privacy and Security > Active Sessions).
  2. Look at every session in the list. You should recognise each one — your own phone, your own laptop. If you see a device, app, or location you do not recognise, tap it and choose Terminate Session immediately.
  3. If you find a session you do not recognise, also change your two-step verification password and check your privacy settings, because someone had access to your account.
  4. Use Terminate All Other Sessions if you are unsure — it logs out every device except the one you are holding, and you can log back in on your own devices afterwards.

Make this a weekly habit — the same way you check your bank balance. A session you did not create is proof your account was accessed, even if nothing else looks wrong.

Step 4: Lock down your privacy settings

Your privacy settings decide what strangers can see and do to you on Telegram. Go to Settings > Privacy and Security and set these:

  • Phone number: "My Contacts" (or "Nobody"). Your phone number is the key to your account. Strangers do not need to see it, and hiding it makes it harder for scammers to target you or impersonate you. Set "Who can see my phone number" to "My Contacts".
  • Last seen and online: "My Contacts" or "Nobody". Scammers watch "last seen" to find active targets. Strangers do not need to know when you are online.
  • Profile photos: "My Contacts". Your photo is used to impersonate you — a scammer copies your photo and your name, then messages your family asking for money. Keeping your photo visible only to contacts makes this harder.
  • Groups and channels: "My Contacts". This stops strangers adding you to scam groups — fake investment groups, prize groups, and crypto groups are a major problem in Pakistan. With this setting, a stranger must send you an invite instead of adding you directly.
  • Calls: "My Contacts". Only people you know should be able to call you on Telegram. This blocks most spam and scam calls.
  • Forwarded messages: "My Contacts" or "Nobody". This stops strangers from tracing your messages back to your account when someone forwards them.
  • Block and report, don't argue. If a stranger messages you with a prize, a job offer, or a threat, do not reply. Tap their name, choose Block, and tap Report Spam. Reporting helps Telegram ban the account. Our fake job offers guide explains the job-scam version of this trick in detail.

Step 5: Spot the scams that target Telegram users

Most Telegram scams follow a small number of scripts. Once you know them, they are easy to spot:

  • Fake channels that copy real ones. A channel named almost like a famous news channel, a crypto project, or a celebrity — with the same logo and a similar name — posts "exclusive" links and giveaways. Check the subscriber count, the channel's age, and whether the real project links to it from its official website. Real channels are usually verified with a blue checkmark. Never trust a channel just because it looks familiar.
  • Crypto and "doubling" giveaways. "Send 0.1 BTC and get 0.2 back" or "Elon Musk is giving away crypto". No one gives away free crypto. Any message asking you to send money to receive more money is a scam — the money you send is gone forever. Our crypto investment scams guide explains this trick in full.
  • Fake Telegram Premium and "free gifts". "You won 1 year of Telegram Premium" or "Claim your free gift" — with a link that asks for your login code or a small payment. Telegram Premium is bought inside the official app, never through a random link. Delete the message.
  • Fake job offers and "online earning" groups. A message offers easy work from home, then asks for a "registration fee" or your bank details. Real employers do not hire over Telegram and never ask for a fee.
  • Investment and trading groups. You are added to a group where everyone claims to be making money from crypto or forex. The "winners" are fake accounts run by the scammers. The moment you deposit money, it is gone.
  • Impersonation — "your son/daughter needs money". A scammer copies your photo and name, creates a fake account, and messages your family asking for emergency money. Before sending any money, call the person on their real number. A voice or video call takes thirty seconds and stops this scam completely.

The rule for all of these is the same: slow down. Scammers create urgency — "now", "today only", "limited offer" — because a person in a hurry does not think. If a message makes you feel rushed or frightened, stop, and check it through a different channel: call the person, visit the official website yourself, or ask a family member.

Step 6: Use Secret Chats, a passcode lock, and auto-delete

Telegram has extra protections for your most private conversations. Use them:

  • Secret Chats for sensitive conversations. A normal Telegram chat is stored on Telegram's servers. A Secret Chat is end-to-end encrypted, lives only on the two phones in the conversation, and cannot be forwarded. To start one, open a contact's profile, tap the three dots, and choose Start Secret Chat. Use Secret Chats for anything you would not want anyone else to read.
  • Turn on auto-delete. In any chat, tap the contact or group name, choose Auto-Delete, and pick 24 hours, 7 days, or 1 month. Messages delete themselves automatically — less history for anyone who grabs your phone to read. You can also set a default auto-delete timer for all new chats in Settings > Privacy and Security > Auto-Delete Messages.
  • Lock Telegram with a passcode. In Settings > Privacy and Security > Passcode Lock, set a passcode (and fingerprint if your phone supports it). Telegram will lock itself when you leave it, so a thief with your phone still cannot read your chats.
  • Set "Delete my account if away for" to 6 months. In Settings > Privacy and Security, Telegram can delete your account automatically if you do not use it for a set time. Six months is a safe choice — if you lose your phone and cannot recover it, your old account and messages will not sit there forever for someone else to abuse.

Step 7: Only use the official Telegram app

Modified Telegram apps are one of the biggest risks on the platform — and one of the easiest to avoid:

  • Install only from the official stores. Google Play Store, Apple App Store, or telegram.org. If a website offers a Telegram APK download, do not use it — our fake apps guide explains how lookalike apps steal your account.
  • Never install a "modded" Telegram. Apps that promise hidden features — seeing deleted messages, downloading restricted media, or unlimited accounts — are a common way to steal your login session. The moment you sign in to a modded app, the thief has your session.
  • Keep Telegram updated. Updates fix security holes. Turn on automatic updates for Telegram in your app store, or check for updates every week.
  • Check app permissions. Telegram needs contacts, camera, and storage to work. It does not need access to your SMS messages, your call logs, or the ability to install other apps. If an app calling itself Telegram asks for those, it is fake — uninstall it immediately.

Your account was stolen — recover it now

If you are locked out of Telegram, act quickly — the thief is messaging your contacts right now. Do these steps in order:

  1. Log in again with your number. Open Telegram, enter your phone number, and enter the login code sent by SMS. Logging in does not automatically log the thief out — so go straight to Step 2.
  2. Terminate all other sessions. Go to Settings > Devices and tap Terminate All Other Sessions. This logs the thief out of your account on every device except yours.
  3. Turn on two-step verification immediately. If you did not have it on, set it now (Step 1). If the thief set a two-step password you do not know, you cannot reset it from inside the app — you must wait for the account to be reset through Telegram's recovery process, or wait out the lockout period.
  4. Warn your contacts immediately. As soon as you are back in, post in your groups and message your family: "My Telegram was stolen. Do not send money or click links from my account before today." The thief's most common trick is asking your contacts for emergency money in your name.
  5. Check your groups and channels. If you run a channel or group, check that the thief did not add themselves as an admin, post scam links, or change the channel details. Remove any admin you do not recognise and delete scam posts.
  6. Contact Telegram support. If you still cannot recover the account, contact Telegram support from inside the app (Settings > Ask a Question) or at telegram.org/support. Explain what happened, with your phone number in full international format (for example, +92 3XX XXXXXXX). Be aware that Telegram support is slow and mostly automated — the fastest recovery is logging in yourself and terminating the thief's sessions.
  7. Report the crime. In Pakistan, Telegram hijacking and the fraud that follows it can be reported to the FIA Cybercrime Wing. Keep screenshots of the scam messages, the thief's username, and any money-transfer details. Our guide to reporting cybercrime and online scams gives the exact steps and the evidence to keep.

Key Takeaways

  • Turn on two-step verification today — a password plus a recovery email stops most Telegram theft. It takes less than a minute.
  • Never share your Telegram login code with anyone, for any reason. Telegram will never ask for it, and no real friend needs it.
  • Check your active sessions every week and terminate anything you do not recognise.
  • Set your privacy to "My Contacts" for your phone number, last seen, profile photo, groups and calls.
  • Only install the official Telegram app — never a modded APK or a lookalike from a random website.
  • Slow down when a message creates urgency — verify through a voice or video call before you send money or share a code.
  • If your account is stolen, log in again, terminate all other sessions, and warn your contacts — then report it to Telegram and the FIA.
  • Everything here is defensive and legal — protecting your own account, your own chats, and your own family from scammers.
