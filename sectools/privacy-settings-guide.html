Privacy Settings Guide

Default social-media settings usually favor sharing, not privacy. Pick a platform below and work through what to review. Note: apps rename menus often — if a setting moved, search the app's settings for the keyword.

Ethical Use Only: These tools are for defensive purposes — securing your own accounts and systems. Never use security knowledge against systems you do not own or lack written permission to test.
The mindset:

Ask three questions about everything you post: who can see this, what could a stranger infer from it (location, routine, contacts), and how long will it exist? Attackers build spear-phishing profiles from exactly these public crumbs — birthday posts, pet names (common passwords!), check-ins, and workplace details.

About This Tool

What this tool does

The Privacy Settings Guide walks you through the privacy controls on the platforms and devices you use every day: social media accounts, smartphones, browsers, and messaging apps. Most services default to sharing more than you would choose — your profile, location, contacts, and activity — because data collection is their business model.

The guide is organized by platform, with concrete steps: who can see your posts, whether your location is attached to photos, which apps have access to your microphone and contacts, and how to limit ad tracking. Each section takes only a few minutes.

It is for everyone who uses a smartphone or social media — which is nearly everyone. This is defensive privacy hygiene: reducing what you broadcast makes you a harder target for scammers, stalkers, and identity thieves, and it simply gives you back control of your own information.

How to use it

  1. Start with your most-used social account. Open its privacy settings (not just profile settings) and review every section.
  2. Set your posts and profile to friends-only or private, and review your follower list for accounts you do not recognize.
  3. Turn off location tagging on posts and photos, and set location access for apps to "only while using" at most.
  4. Review which apps and websites are connected to your social accounts, and revoke access for anything you no longer use.
  5. On your phone, audit app permissions: camera, microphone, contacts, and location should go only to apps that genuinely need them.
  6. Repeat quarterly, and after every major app update — updates sometimes reset or add new sharing options.

Defensive security tips

  • Defaults are not your friend. Assume every new app and account shares the maximum until you change it. Check settings on day one.
  • Limit location sharing. Real-time location shared with the wrong person is a physical safety issue, not just a privacy preference.
  • Audit third-party access. Old quiz apps and long-deleted services often retain access to your account data. Revoke what you do not use.
  • Think before you post. Vacation announcements, children's schools, and visible documents in photos are gifts to scammers and burglars. Settings help, but discretion helps more.
  • Use the platform's download-your-data tool. Seeing what a company holds on you is the fastest way to understand why these settings matter.

FAQs

Will tightening privacy settings break my apps?

Rarely. Most privacy settings only limit sharing and tracking, not core features. If something stops working, you can re-enable that specific permission — the guide notes which settings are safe to change.

Is private mode on social media really private?

It limits who among other users can see your content, but the platform itself still collects your data. Private mode protects you from strangers and scammers, not from the company's own data collection.

How often should I review these settings?

Every few months, and after major app updates. Companies periodically redesign settings pages and occasionally reset choices, so a quick quarterly check keeps you in control.

What is the single most impactful privacy change?

Reviewing app permissions on your phone — especially location, contacts, and microphone. Your phone's sensors reveal more about your life than any single social media setting.
